## Abstract

Hydraulic systems are everywhere — excavators, car brakes, dentist chairs — but most students never touch one until university. Our goal was to build a working hydraulic arm from materials available at any hardware shop in Kathmandu, for less than the price of a textbook.

The final arm uses four syringe pairs (10 mL for the gripper and wrist, 20 mL for the two main joints) connected by vinyl tubing filled with water. Total build cost: approximately NPR 1,400.

**Result:** the arm lifts a 300 g water bottle through its full range with no leaks over 50+ operating cycles. Larger 20 mL control syringes move slower but exert more force — a tradeoff we only understood after our first two designs failed.
